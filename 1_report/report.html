Formosa Telecom: listen to what customers tell support

DeployAI 2026 · Commercial proposal for Vivian Hsu, CMO · data to 30 June 2026

1. Executive summary

The finding that changes the plan. When a customer tells a support agent they are thinking of leaving, about half of them are gone within 60 days (49.3% vs 3.7% for all postpaid customers, tested on three past dates using only notes written before each date). Formosa's current renewal push, aimed at contracts ending within four months, reaches people who leave at 4.2%: barely above average. Nobody reads these notes today, and 638 of them were not even tagged as a cancellation risk.
NT$3.7M–NT$13.1Ma year, keep customers who say they may leave
NT$2.2M–NT$2.6Ma year, sell only what customers ask for
NT$1.4M–NT$4.2Ma year, move paid spend to the cheapest channel

Net margin at Formosa's full scale (sample ×130), 60% margin, six months of credited revenue per customer as Finance counts it. Ranges and assumptions in Part 4.

1 · Reduce churn

Do: read every support note each week, flag postpaid customers who say they may leave, and answer their reason with the approved offer that fits it (Renewal discount, Data boost or Device credit), calling the highest-bill customers first. Billing errors and network problems get fixed, not discounted. Worth: NT$3.7M to NT$13.1M net margin a year, keeping 2,566–8,981 customers.

2 · Earn more from existing customers

Do: stop roaming blasts: against their control group they caused no extra sales (NT$2.0M a year). Broad upsell blasts report 3.5× but really return 1.6×, below break-even on margin. Send upgrade, data add-on and roaming offers only to customers who asked for them in a note. Worth: NT$2.2M to NT$2.6M a year, mostly from spend no longer wasted.

3 · Win new customers

Do: shift paid budget toward Google Search (cheapest line, NT$1,499) and away from Retail Promotion (NT$1,610); every paid channel takes about six months to pay back, and 2026 campaigns pay back slower than 2025. Add a welcome check-in: customers in their first six months leave at 9.5% in 60 days, 2.6× the average. Worth: NT$1.4M to NT$4.2M more margin a year at NT$20–60M of paid spend.

The one decision: approve a 90-day retention pilot for postpaid customers who tell support they may leave. Expected cost NT$1.1M (at most NT$1.9M, inside the NT$2.5M budget). One in five is held back at random to prove the result. It pays for itself if it keeps 1.3% of would-be leavers, and can prove a save rate of 6.2% or more. Paid offers stop at day 45 if contacted customers leave as often as held-back ones.

Values we could not trust, and what we did

  • Latest values only. Plan, contract end and tenure describe 1 July (or the last day), not the past. We never use contract end or plan to pick customers in the backtest except for the comparison rule, and tenure is recounted to each test date.
  • data_gb = 999 means unlimited; it is a flag, not 999 GB. 750 customers have no allowance recorded and are treated as unknown.
  • Vague campaign targets. 42 past campaigns were briefed as "TBD", "everyone" or "not yet defined".
  • Checks that keep problems out: every run re-counts rows at each step; any note sentence the AI has not read sends that note to a review queue as "unknown" instead of guessing.

3. Analysis

Postpaid churn runs at about 1.8% a month, as the fact sheet says. What customers tell support is by far the strongest early warning in the data: customers whose notes show leave intent leave 13× more often than average. New customers (first six months) are the second warning. Price and rival offers are the main reason flagged customers give. Past campaigns overstate their effect: once compared with the control group, roaming adds nothing and upsell returns less than half of what was reported.

Why they leave

What customers say about rivals

Notes name a rival 417 times. Customers who name one left at 53%–62% in January–June, against 12% for all customers. More than a third are already acting (a quote, a store visit, a porting request), so speed matters more than the size of the offer. Where the rival simply offers more for the money, Data boost (NT$300) is the cheapest approved answer.

4. Interpretation: what it is worth

5. Campaign engine

Audiences are picked by written rules in code (signals.py), refreshed whenever a new notes file arrives. Every message is drafted from approved offers only, gets the legal footer added by code, and passes an automatic compliance check. 89 of 240 past campaigns would have failed that check.

QA checklist before any send

  • Offer is on the approved list for this objective; no free months, cash or bill waivers.
  • Legal footer verbatim as the closing paragraph (short terms line on SMS and LINE only).
  • No banned claim or close paraphrase ("never drops", "cheapest", "fastest network" unqualified…).
  • Audience excludes family, business, prepaid (for retention) and "no save" lines; 20% held back at random.
  • Outbound calls within 2,000 a month; prices in NT$; copy in English.

6. Measurement and the pilot

Past owned campaigns earned less than reported: against the group held back on purpose, roaming caused nothing and upsell caused 44% of what it claimed. The pilot is built so this mistake cannot happen again: 20% held back at random from day one, success measured as the gap in churn between the two groups.

The 90-day pilot

ItemPlan
WhoPostpaid basic and premium customers in tiers R1 and R2, refreshed weekly from new notes. About 13,000 customers over 90 days at full scale.
Held back2,600 (one in five, at random). They get nothing.
Channels6,000 outbound calls (2,000 a month, highest bill first); everyone else LINE and SMS.
OffersRenewal discount, Data boost or Device credit by reason; billing and network problems fixed, not discounted.
CostNT$1.1M expected (messages NT$25,029, offers NT$1.0M, creative NT$69,291); at most NT$1.9M. Hard cap NT$2.5M: offers stop when the cap is reached.
ExpectedKeep 513–1,796 customers; credited revenue NT$2.1M–NT$7.3M.
Break-evenKeep 1.3% of would-be leavers: churn in the contacted group 0.62 percentage points below the held-back group.
What it can proveWith 2,600 held back, chance alone moves the gap by up to ±2.1 percentage points. The pilot can reliably prove a gap of 3.1 percentage points or more, i.e. a save rate of 6.2% or more (95% confidence, 80% power). A save rate between 1.3% and 6.2% would still make money but cannot be proven in 90 days.
Day 45 checkStop paid offers (keep care calls) if the contacted group's churn is not below the held-back group's. At day 45 noise is about ±3.0 percentage points, so this checks the direction only, not the size.
Day 90 verdictGap of 3.1 percentage points or more: proven, scale to the full base. Gap above zero but smaller: profitable but unproven, extend 90 days to add customers. Gap at or below zero: stop.
MeasureChurn within 60 days of contact, contacted vs held back; offer cost per kept customer; take-up by offer and channel.

Risks

  • Save rate unknown. Formosa has never run reason-matched retention. The held-back group measures it, but only effects of 6% or more can be proven in 90 days.
  • Offers taken by people who would have stayed. Costed at 1.5 takers per customer kept.
  • Coverage. Notes catch about 9% of leavers. Next phase: the R3 welcome check-in, and asking agents to log every contact.
  • Call centre capacity. 2,000 calls a month; the rest go to LINE and SMS, so the result is measured by channel.

How we used AI

Claude Opus 5.5 read the 403 distinct sentences that make up all 8,000 notes once and labelled each with a topic and a leave-intent level; it also wrote the message templates. The code only looks these up, so the results rerun offline. See 3_ai_outputs/how_we_did_it.md.
